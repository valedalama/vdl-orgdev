OrgMap Field Guide

One renderer, three organisations, several ways of reading each. This is the guide to what the maps say, who they are for, and what the software will not let you claim.

Live, and still moving. The Vale da Lama map is published at valedalama.github.io/vdl-orgdev and runs the same renderer described here. Design decisions marked open below are genuinely open — where a choice has not been made, this guide says so rather than pretending otherwise.

01What this is

A tool for mapping the social system of an organisation, or of a community of organisations, from a data file anyone can read.

Most org charts are drawings. Somebody arranges boxes, and the arrangement becomes the claim — who matters, who reports to whom, who is at the centre. When the organisation changes, the drawing is redrawn, and the reasoning behind the first version is lost.

OrgMap is built the other way round. There is a data file that records what is true, and a schema that says what kinds of thing may be recorded and how each is drawn. The picture is generated. Change the data and the picture follows; change the schema and the same data is read differently, with nothing edited at all.

Three organisations run in it today, and they are as different as three can be — a single farm's internal governance, a bioregional network of independent land projects, and a small worked example that is neither. The renderer contains the words "circle", "role" and "member" nowhere in its code. The schema tells it what things are; the code never knows.

02Two products, one file

The same code runs in two modes, for two audiences. Knowing which one you are looking at explains most of what you can and cannot do.

The MapThe Workbench
Whoeveryone in the organisationthe one or two people doing the modelling
Wherepublished — a public or private web addresslocal, or a private URL
Can edit?No. There is no editor and no ExportYes — rows, and the schema files
Remembers you?No. It writes nothing to your browserYes — your edits and your layout

A deployment declares which it is, in a file called manifest.json, along with its title, its datasets, and any banner or links it wants in the header. Add ?mode=view to any address to see the published version; ?mode=workbench for the editor.

Why it matters

The Vale da Lama map names real colleagues. A published map that shipped an editor would invite someone to change the organisation's record by clicking on it — so in view mode the editor is not hidden, it is removed, and there is no path by which the page can write anything at all.

03The one idea

Three moves. Everything else in the tool follows from them.

Attributes are not relationships

Being in the same valley is a fact about each organisation, not a line between them. Three organisations in Bensafrim should not produce a triangle. Belonging to a community is the same kind of fact. When these are drawn as lines they swamp the map — and they swamp it in a way that looks like a finding rather than an artefact.

A relationship is a claim, and it is typed

Every line names its own kind, as a predicate — a stable web address that explains what the line means. A line is not "a connection"; it is this organisation declares an affiliation with that one, or this role sits in that circle. The type is data, not styling, so the map can be filtered, checked, and argued with.

Views are filters over one dataset

The public map, a regional view, a governance view: all of them are the same rows read through different rules. There is never a second picture maintained by hand somewhere else.

04Reading a map

The legend, once. It is the same on every map — only the vocabulary changes.

Circles, lines — and beads

Where a map draws a channel (see below), the role that carries it rides the line as a small bead. A bead says who holds that position, and whether the organisation has actually decided so:

BeadMeans
Initialssomeone holds it, and a ratification date is on record
Initials + ?someone holds it, but no ratification is recorded
?vacant — the seat exists and nobody is in it
Hollow, dottedno record — the seat was never created
The distinction that took longest to get right

Vacant and absent are different failures. A vacant seat is a job waiting for someone. An absent one was never written down, so nothing renders it — and nothing reads as fine. The ghost bead exists because a map that draws nothing where a seat should be will be read as a map with no problem there.

What a line's record tells you

More than the line can. Both ends, the predicate spelled out, who declared it, whether both sides did, whether it is confirmed, where the row came from — and two things worth naming separately:

  • Runs through. Where a relationship exists because of a particular person, the record names them. The shared-personnel line between Mud Valley Foundation and Quinta Vale da Lama runs through Walt Ludwick, and the record says so.
  • What this publishes. The exact two-field entry the subject would carry in its own Murmurations profile — predicate and object, and nothing else. Set beside the full record, it shows the line that matters: who the relationship runs through is recorded locally and is not part of the published claim.

Also worth knowing

  • Hover a circle for everything recorded about it. Hover a line for both ends, the predicate, who declared it, and any note. Lines are thin, so each carries a wide invisible catch area — you do not have to hit the stroke.
  • Click anything to open its full record on the right. Hovering is a glance; clicking is the record, and it stays open while you look elsewhere. Everything the selection is not about dims. The panel walks — click any connection to move there.
  • Find in the header dims what does not match. It never hides, so a filter can never be mistaken for an absence.
  • Drag a circle out of a tangle; scroll to zoom; drag the background to pan. Beads ride their own line and are moved by moving the circles.
  • The number under a circle counts the lines currently drawn — so it changes when you change the view. That is correct, not a glitch.

05If you are new at Vale da Lama

For someone joining the farm

Vale da Lama runs on Holacracy: work is organised into circles rather than departments, and people fill roles inside them. The map exists so you can see the shape of that without reading the whole governance handbook first.

The map is at valedalama.github.io/vdl-orgdev.

Find yourself in four steps

  1. Type your name into Find. Everything that is not you dims.
  2. The circles you are attached to are the ones still lit. Click your name for your full record — every role you energize, and which circle each sits in.
  3. Hover any circle to read its purpose — the one sentence saying why it exists. Those sentences are the fastest orientation to the farm there is.
  4. Switch Schema to double link to see how your circle connects to the one above it.

What the map tells you

There are nine circles: VdL Farm, Farm Ops, Casa Ops, Groups & Retreats, Activities, B&B Accommodations, Restaurant, OMG (Organic Market Garden), and HFC (Holistic Farm Care). It holds 28 roles across them, and 14 people.

A person is not a job title here. People energize roles, and several people hold more than one — Anna Pelegrim is Secretary of Farm Ops and Circle Rep of OMG. The map draws that as two roles, because it is two roles.

What it shows that no previous version could

Nine seats are empty, and you can see them. A role exists whether or not anybody holds it, so an unfilled position is a mark on the map rather than a silence. Activities has no Lead, no Rep and no Secretary. Farm Ops' Circle Rep is open pending election.

And nineteen roles are held by someone with no ratification on record. That is not an accusation — it means the governance decision was never written down. Each amber ? disappears as a date is recorded.

06If you are new to the Barlavento community

For a member organisation

Barlavento is not one organisation. It is a set of independent land projects, farms, and initiatives in the western Algarve, each of which owns its own story. The map is an attempt to show how they connect without any one of them getting to narrate the others.

See the community in three steps

  1. Set Dataset to Barlavento community.
  2. Start on as recorded today. One organisation sits in the middle, touching all 13 others.
  3. Switch to vocabulary.md. Nothing in the data changed — but the map now draws 5 lines instead of 23, and the centre is gone.

Of the 23 recorded connections, 12 were one organisation recording that it convenes everybody, and 6 were "these two are in the same place." Eighteen of twenty-three lines were facts about single organisations, drawn as if they were relationships. Once they move onto the circles where they belong, the honest size of the recorded relationship graph is five.

The rule that matters

Nothing appears on the map that its subject did not choose to say. That is the direction of travel, and it is why the tool tracks where every row came from.

How to put yourself on the map properly

  1. Publish a Murmurations profile on your own website, carrying the barlavento-eco tag.
  2. Your location and address are already ordinary profile fields — do not record them as connections to anybody.
  3. To declare a relationship, add an entry to your profile's relationships array with a predicate and the other organisation's web address.
  4. Two predicates exist today: https://schema.org/affiliation, and the community's own …/predicates/shared-personnel/.

Both sides declaring the same link makes it mutual, and the map draws it thicker. One side is enough to draw it at all.

On naming people

The shared-personnel claim deliberately does not name the person. "We share personnel with X" is your organisation's claim to make. Naming an individual is that individual's.

07Administering the Vale da Lama data

The map is not the source of truth. Notion is.

The chain runs: Notion holds the circles, people and roles → scripts/build-graph.mjs turns them into graph.json → the map reads that file. A GitHub Action runs the middle step every morning and commits the result only when something changed, so the file's history is the organisation's structural audit trail.

To change what the map says

Change it in Notion. The map catches up within a day, or immediately if you run the workflow by hand. There is nowhere else to edit it — the published map has no editor.

To change what the map is allowed to say

Edit a file in schemas/. Adding a node kind, a predicate, or an allowed value is a few lines. That is also how you propose a change to the model without touching a single row of data.

Recording a decision

Two date fields on every Role: Ratified On and Review Date. Blank does not mean "not ratified" — it means no record of ratification exists. Today that is the state of all nineteen occupied roles, which is why every held bead wears an amber ?. Fill the date in after a Governance Meeting and the badge clears.

Validation sheets

A circle validates its own record at its own meeting. docs/generated/validation/ holds one sheet per circle, in Portuguese and English, listing the four core roles, the defined roles, and a to check section of everything a human must decide. Regenerate with node scripts/build-validation-sheets.mjs after any change. Across the nine circles it currently flags 24 items and 16 core seats with no record.

Handle with care

This map names real colleagues and shows where each of them sits. It is published, by a deliberate decision that Vale da Lama is transparent about its governance. There is no privacy layer — everyone who has the address can see every name.

08Administering the Barlavento data

Two sources, and one job: make the second one disappear.

Every row carries a source:

ValueMeansWhat to do with it
self‑publishedRead from the member's own Murmurations profile Nothing. It maintains itself.
curatedRecorded about the member by somebody else, from the spreadsheet Help that member publish it themselves, then delete the row.

Today every row is curated. Nobody in the original spreadsheet declared anything about themselves. So the source column is a ledger of unfinished business, and the map's honesty improves as it empties. Hovering any line shows that row's provenance, so the ledger can be audited from the map rather than the table.

Adding a member

You do not. They publish a profile with the barlavento-eco tag and the index finds them. If you must record something ahead of that, mark it curated, and mark it candidate if you are not certain — an unverified guess about a real organisation must never render as a fact.

Adding a new kind of relationship

A predicate is just a stable web address that explains itself. Open a pull request adding a page under docs/predicates/, with a fixed permalink. It must say what the claim means, what it does not claim, and the nearest standard term. A predicate address is a permanent commitment — it can be clarified, never repurposed or deleted.

Deliberately absent

The original spreadsheet had a contact column naming an individual at each of the 14 organisations. It is not carried into this data: nothing in the map needs it, and those people did not opt in.

09The data models

Two files per organisation — things and relationships — and a schema that governs both. This is the part most worth arguing with.

Barlavento — the four kinds

Nodes are Project, Initiative, Network or Convener. The four recorded kinds of connection split two and two, and that split is the entire difference between its two schemas:

KindRowsUnder vocabulary.mdDrawn as
Membership12attributea halo on the circle
Co‑location6attributecolour — already a field on every node
Affiliation3relationshipa line · schema.org/affiliation
Shared personnel2relationshipa line · the community's own predicate

Vale da Lama — the reified role

Nodes are Circle, Role and Person. Instead of a person connecting to a circle, a person fills a role, and the role sits in the circle:

RelationshipFrom → toStandard term
Sub‑circlecircle → circleorg:subOrganizationOf
Role in its circlerole → circleorg:postIn
Fills the roleperson → roleorg:holds
Member, no role recordedperson → circle— a record disagreement, off by default

A role that exists whether or not anybody holds it is a standard idea — the W3C Organization Ontology calls it a Post, and that class exists for exactly this reason. Reifying the relationship is what lets an empty position be visible instead of rendering as nothing.

Channels — when two roles are one mechanism

A schema may declare a channel: a relationship derived by composing two others, drawn as an arc rather than as separate lines. Vale da Lama's is the Holacracy double link.

A Circle Lead is appointed by the circle above and carries its purpose down — solidarity. A Circle Rep is elected by the circle's members and carries tensions up — subsidiarity. Together they are one two-way channel, and the map draws them as a pair with the role riding its own arc. Where one half is vacant the channel is drawn broken, because a circle with no Rep has no way to speak upward.

Nothing about this is Holacracy-specific. Sociocracy's operational leader and elected representative are the same two arcs with different labels; only the strings in the schema change.

How the schema stops you

  • The editor only offers what is legal. Every governed field is a dropdown built from the schema. Under vocabulary.md the relationship editor offers two predicates; co-location is listed but cannot be chosen, because it is not a relationship.
  • Validate checks the whole file. Ten rules, each naming itself: unknown predicate, wrong kind of thing at either end of a line, missing id, a line to nowhere, a line to itself, a duplicate claim, a value outside a controlled list, a deprecated predicate, and rows absorbed as attributes rather than drawn.

The rule carrying the most weight is the one about the ends of a line. Fills the role may only point at a role — so a person connected straight to a circle is refused rather than quietly drawn.

The dials

  • Draw — a switch per relationship kind. Turning them off one at a time is the fastest way to see what any single kind contributes.
  • Colour by and Badge by — node kind, or an attribute. Both hide themselves when they cannot change anything in the current view: a control that does nothing invites a click and teaches that the app is broken.
  • Find — dims what does not match.
  • Labels — names on or off; whether mutual pairs draw thicker.
  • Re-derive layout — throw the arrangement away and let it settle again.

Giving the map the screen

mMap only — hides the panels and the controls
pPanels on or off (workbench only)
cControls on or off
fFullscreen
escClose the record panel — or, with none open, bring it all back
gOpen this field guide

A big change of size re-derives the layout so the graph fills the space; a small one leaves the arrangement you were reading alone.

11Running and deploying

Looking at a published map

Open its address. Vale da Lama's is valedalama.github.io/vdl-orgdev. Nothing to install, nothing to log into, and it will not remember you.

Running the workbench

./run.shServes it at http://localhost:8090/
scripts/seed.pyRebuilds the sample datasets from their real sources
./deploy‑to‑mini.shPushes a copy to the always-on machine

Opening the file directly will not work — the page fetches its schemas and data, and browsers block that for local files. Your edits live in your own browser; Reset to seed discards them, and Export hands them back as files you can commit.

Deploying a map for your own organisation

  1. Fork the repository.
  2. Put your things and relationships into two CSVs. A spreadsheet export is fine. Things need id, name, nodeType; relationships need subject, predicate, object. Everything else is optional and is kept if present.
  3. Write a schema declaring your node kinds, your predicates, and which kinds of thing each predicate may join. Copy schemas/example.json and edit it.
  4. Write a manifest.json: your title, "mode": "view", your dataset, and any banner or links for the header.
  5. Look at the map. Argue. Go back to step 3. This is the actual work, and it is governance work rather than software work.
  6. Turn on Pages.
  7. Optional, and last: write a loader so the CSVs regenerate from wherever your truth already lives. An organisation whose structure changes twice a year never needs this.
Putting a map inside a repository you already have

Forking gets you everything. If instead you are dropping a map into an existing site, copy five things into one folder and nothing else: index.html, manifest.json, your schemas/, your data, and vendor/d3.min.js.

The vendored copy of d3 is the one people forget — we forgot it. The map then loads looking almost right: the title, the banner and the dataset switcher all appear, and the canvas is simply empty. The console says ReferenceError: d3 is not defined. Nothing else produces that particular half-working page.

What a deployment declares

manifest.json carries the title, the mode, the datasets and where each one's data comes from — a pair of CSVs, or a single JSON file shaped either {nodes, edges} or {nodes, relationships}. Both are read; a loader is never asked to adopt the other one's key names.

Adding an organisation touches no code. That is not an aspiration, and it is no longer only a test: the Vale da Lama governance map and the barlavento.eco community map run the same index.html, byte for byte. One draws a Holacracy holarchy from a Notion database; the other draws a peer network of independent farms from profiles those farms publish on their own domains. What differs between them is a manifest, two schema files and the data.

12What is provisional — and what to tell us

Open, by design

  • Every visual treatment. How a channel, a vacancy or a confirmed pair should look is a design decision. What is in front of you reads clearly enough to argue about.
  • Whether four kinds are enough for Barlavento. They are the four found in the recorded data — not a considered taxonomy.
  • Photographs on beads. The code supports them; nobody has uploaded one, so every bead shows initials. Two letters where we have a full name, one where we do not.

Known noise

  • Every Vale da Lama node has status "Active", so colouring by status shows a single colour — which is why that control now hides itself there.
  • Membership is on all 14 Barlavento nodes, so as a badge it distinguishes nobody. It earns its halo once non-members appear.
  • Purpose and themes are free text. Useful on hover, uncontrolled.
  • Three "member, no role recorded" lines in the Vale da Lama data are two records disagreeing about who is in a circle. They are off by default and are a question for a Governance Meeting, not a bug.

What would help most

  1. Open the map that concerns you and try to answer a question you actually have. Tell us the question, not the bug.
  2. Name a field you would delete, and one you wish existed.
  3. If a line about your organisation is wrong, say so — that is data, and the fastest kind of fix.
